# Forecasting

The process of projecting future developments or conditions based on the analysis of past and present data and observations.

- It involves identifying trends, patterns and relationships within the data to make informed projections.
- Forecasting is entirely extrapolative in nature and typically assumes that an observed relationship will continue into the future.
- Forecasting is useful when trying to understand an environment that is relatively stable and where you have a good understanding of the variables in play.

## Blind forecasts

- Consider a turkey that is fed every day. Every feeding firms up its belief that being fed by friendly humans is the general rule of life, until the afternoon before the feast, when it incurs a revision of belief. This is presupposing that a sequence of events in the future will occur as it always has in the past: a blind forecast. A chart of the turkey's well-being rises steadily for a thousand days and then drops to zero. This is what non-linearity looks like.
- We are getting worse at making forecasts because the world is getting more complex, not merely more complicated. A small change in a few variables can make predictions almost impossibly complex; economists have failed to predict 148 of the past 153 recessions. Complicated problems require expert knowledge and can be analysed to a predictable answer. In complex problems cause-and-effect relationships are not predictable or fully understood, the system and its dynamics are constantly changing, and outcomes are emergent and only apparent in hindsight.

## The limits of forecasting

- Forecasting gives value in a relatively stable environment where the variables in play are well understood. As the complexity and uncertainty of the external environment grow, predetermination decreases and forecasting gives a false sense of knowing, while the value of exploring plausibility, challenging assumptions and acknowledging emergence, rises.
- No timescale can be put on this, because the decreasing power of predetermination varies from industry to industry and within one industry over time. In very long-term futures foresight too loses its grip, because there are too many unknown unknowns.
- Prediction and forecast work when patterns hold; foresight helps when they break. Foresight exists to challenge the tendency to linear thinking and to question assumptions that become misguided or obsolete as reality shifts.
