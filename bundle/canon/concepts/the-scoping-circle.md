# The Scoping Circle

California 100 and School of International Futures have developed a powerful yet simple approach to scoping a foresight project which they call the Scoping Circle. It is a set of six steps that can be used to get a clear understanding of your question, purpose, context and approach.

## How it works

- You start with the question that you have the clearest answer to and work your way around the circle as many times as is useful.
- Using it iteratively helps you refine your thinking.
- You should always consider including different perspectives in the scoping exercise, such as the people/team who asked for the project, project beneficiaries, and other relevant individuals or groups. This helps to ensure that the project stakeholders agree on the scope and boundaries of the project.

## Figure: the Scoping Circle

A hand-drawn circular arrow at the centre, with six labelled steps arranged around it, three on the left and three on the right, indicating an iterative loop through the following questions.

- Define the focal question (top left): What question do you want the project to answer? What is the core issue of interest? Where will you draw the project boundaries? What is the challenge that needs a response?
- Understand the context (top right): What system does this project exist in? What is the project seeking to achieve? What needs to be considered to ensure impact?
- Engage stakeholders (middle left): Who is the project for? What needs will it satisfy? Which decision should the foresight work influence? Who might be upset or resistant to change? How are they included?
- Design the approach (middle right): What approach/tools best support the purpose and needs of the project? What could go wrong? What should be avoided? What does the foresight work help you do?
- Make the case (bottom left): How will evidence come together to make the case for change? What is important to see for different audiences/users? How can you make the most of the foresight work produced?
- Design the research (bottom right): What kind of future insights will work in this context? Where will you look for future insights and signals of change? Who will you talk to? Who will you collaborate with?
