# Complicated versus complex

Complicated systems can be figured out. Complex systems have to be explored. The distinction, drawn from the Cynefin framework, decides which kind of thinking a problem needs.

| | Clear | Complicated | Complex | Chaotic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Characteristics | Problems are relatively simple and well understood; cause and effect are clear | Problems can be understood and addressed with sufficient analysis and expertise; cause and effect exist but are not immediately obvious; there is a right answer, or a limited set of good answers | Problems and systems are dynamic, unpredictable and constantly changing; traditional expertise has its limits as cause and effect are not predictable or fully knowable; outcomes are emergent and only apparent in hindsight | Problems are urgent; no clear cause and effect upfront; immediate action is required to stabilise the situation |
| How to approach | Predict, or sense and respond | Expert knowledge, detailed analysis | Exploration, systems thinking, lateral thinking | Decisive action to stabilise, followed by analysis |

- Complicated problems are predictable with enough analysis; we can analyse our way out of them.
- In complex problems the system and its dynamics are constantly changing, and outcomes are emergent and only apparent in hindsight.
- Forecasting belongs to the complicated; foresight to the complex. We are getting worse at making forecasts because the world is getting more complex, not merely more complicated.
