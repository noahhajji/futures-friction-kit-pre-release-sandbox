# Futures-driven innovation

Futures-driven innovation enables organisations to move beyond path dependency and act across all three horizons with greater intent. That requires both a clear sense of direction and a sharper understanding of where the most meaningful strategic fits are on the time horizon.

## A shift in mindset

- Rather than innovating forward from the present alone, organisations need to work backwards from the future, asking what it would take to get there.
- Signals and structural shifts are used as inputs informing innovation decisions today, inspired by existing strategic foresight methodologies from scenario-building to horizon scanning to critical uncertainty mapping.
- The purpose is not to invent for a made-up world. It is to use futures thinking to identify where today's models may become insufficient, where new needs are emerging, and where existing assumptions about markets, users, workflows and value creation may need to be challenged.

## The changing role of innovation

- Innovation is not only about producing the next product, service or pilot. It is also about building new organisational muscles: experimentation, scenario-informed decision-making, simulation, and the ability to learn from the edge rather than only from the core.
- Futures-driven innovation is therefore as much about organisational learning and cultural readiness and relevance as it is about invention.

## Across the horizons

- In Horizon 1, futures-driven innovation may improve current workflows through AI-supported research, better sensing, or more adaptive interfaces.
- In Horizon 2, it may involve piloting agentic collaboration, simulation-based decision settings, or new retail experiences built around context and user intent interfaces.
- In Horizon 3, it asks more radical questions. What happens when customers engage through machine-readable environments rather than traditional channels? What happens when AI agents become part of organisational infrastructure? What happens when human verification capabilities are dramatically outpaced by the intelligence of agentic systems? What happens when value shifts from content and product to system, context and model orchestration?
- These are not questions for later. They are questions that shape what capabilities organisations need to start building now. The challenge is to avoid treating all innovation as if it belongs to the same horizon.

## Long-term courage

- Futures-driven innovation depends on long-term courage, the willingness to invest, experiment and act before the outcome is certain.
- Many organisations struggle to create proper space for more transformative work, when profit, political or stakeholder incentives favour the measurable, the immediate and the low-risk.
- Futures-driven innovation challenges that logic by insisting that long-term relevance requires room for uncertainty, room for experimentation, and room for ideas that may initially look misaligned with the immediate present.
