# Future-preparedness and performance

In times of change, it is the organisations with a culture and organisational processes around strategic foresight that perform better. This is known both from research and from first-hand experience.

## Vigilant, vulnerable, in danger, neurotic

A longitudinal study of around 4,000 companies from 2008 to 2015 classified firms by how well their foresight practices fitted their business environment. Vigilant companies had adequate strategic foresight practices for their environment. Vulnerable and in-danger companies fell short on foresight at different levels. Neurotic companies had too much strategic foresight in relation to what their environment required.

| | All firms | Vigilant | Vulnerable | In danger | Neurotic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Average profitability | 12% | 16% | 10% | 10% | 9% |
| Average market capitalisation growth | 25% | 75% | 38% | -1% | -6% |

- Organisations with a structured approach to foresight outperform on both profitability and growth.
- Too much strategic foresight is also harmful, because it diverts too much attention away from the current business.

## The viability question

- Asked how long their business would remain economically viable if it continued on its current path, the share of chief executives answering ten years or less was 39% in 2023, 45% in 2024 and 42% in 2025.
- Six in ten chief executives judge strategic decisions by the result, not by the quality of the process. A predictable process leads to predictable outcomes, and business leaders are not happy with the outcomes and cannot rely on predictability.
