# Uncertainty / uncertainties

Uncertainty is a feature of complex systems and is fundamentally inherent when working with the future. Recognising it highlights the potential for novelty, emergence and discontinuity. It can arise from having limited knowledge about the future, or from future developments being genuinely undetermined, with multiple ways they could plausibly play out.

## Uncertainty compounds over time

- Many future outcomes are contingent on other developments that have not yet played out, and those, in turn, on others still.
- Uncertainty accumulates the further into the future we look, which is part of why the long term cannot simply be extrapolated from the present.

## Uncertainty is different from risk

- Risk refers to situations where the probabilities of different outcomes are known, or can be estimated with some degree of confidence.
- Uncertainty involves situations where those probabilities are unknown, unknowable or cannot be reliably estimated, reflecting a lack of information or predictability about future events or developments, and implying a higher level of ambiguity and complexity.

## Critical uncertainties

- Uncertainty is at the heart of all foresight work, and a fundamental aspect of strategic foresight involves identifying and engaging with critical uncertainties.
- These are defined as high-impact drivers with uncertain outcomes.

## Uncertainty is a feature

- Working strategically with the future is less about identifying trends and development trajectories and much more about grasping and understanding uncertainty. Acknowledging uncertainty is a fundamental premise for making strategic decisions.
- Uncertainty is the very reason we need strategy: without it, a plan to go from A to B would suffice. Uncertainty is not a bug but a feature, and sometimes the future seems to punish certainty. The future turns many 'givens' into uncertainties, and our brains are not really wired for it.
