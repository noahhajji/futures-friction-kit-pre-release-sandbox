# Newsrooms start to explain their AI choices

Examples collected by Rasmus Kleis Nielsen of newsrooms communicating explicitly about what they do with generative AI and why, in both directions.

## Explaining intense use

- A record number of Pulitzer Prize awardees, eight, disclosed AI use in 2026.
- The New York Times published a human-computer interaction paper on the development and use of the Epstein Files Engine, an in-house AI agent running database queries across corpora.
- Bloomberg's AI researchers contributed eight papers to the 2026 Association for Computational Linguistics conference, on subjects from agentic systems to responsible AI and financial language processing.
- Journalists at Die Zeit and Der Spiegel explained how they used AI to build a searchable database of Nazi Party members.

## Explaining limited use

- A manifesto for the romantic school of journalism was launched at the 2026 International Journalism Festival in Perugia by Lea Korsgaard of Zetland and Joshi Herrmann of Mill Media: limited use of generative AI on the editorial side, explicitly communicated, to stand apart from a profession much of the public assumes is already using it intensely.

## What it signals

- These are not only examples of using or not using AI. They are steps of explicitly communicating how it is used, what for and why. The disclosures are largely oriented at fellow professionals, but they contain the kernel of a more explicit communication about what generative AI in news can mean beyond making it cheaper.
- Nielsen finds it hard to identify established publishers that combine intense AI use with explicit communication; these are the nearest examples.
