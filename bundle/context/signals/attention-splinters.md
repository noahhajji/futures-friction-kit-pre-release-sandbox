# Attention splinters

Liquid content can make our attention splinter into each our own small, personalised medium, at the expense of the shared broad narratives and truths that form a foundation for a democratic society in conversation with itself. The single journalistic story, such as the article, loses vitality as a unit; it may become merely an atom in an AI model.

The shared front page is already more myth than reality for many, but it will disappear even more in the liquid and personal era. The media reality is already being atomised, and such a fragmented information landscape makes the shared public conversation harder. Where engagement has been a carrying metric for social media, intimacy may become it for AI.

## Information oracles

That can lead to information oracles that understand exactly you extremely well, but which you do not really understand, because where does the information actually come from? It is hard to fact-check, or to trust, something we do not understand. At the same time such oracles risk isolating each person. They remove the friction that is necessary to understand others' viewpoints, since it is precisely through shared language and meetings that meaning and ethics arise. It is the ultimate possible tool of manipulation. Social media that create unreal narratives help destabilise democracy itself, creating mistrust and eroding the perception of a shared reality, and generative AI fits perfectly into that dynamic. Newer studies also show how people who use AI heavily risk losing learning, because learning requires a certain friction.

## Common ground

The shared front page showed what other people were also following, and so made the shared public conversation more shared. It is decisive that the producers of content also gain access to the demand signal, and that society keeps a kind of common ground. Right now there are no shared frameworks and structures that reward quality in liquid content.

## What it means for news media

The challenges also constitute a potential. Can news media create the shared front pages of the future, or communities where people gather around stories and news? Bundling of content has already proved relatively successful, including shared subscriptions across media. One can also imagine journalistically oriented organisations developing a healthier type of information oracle, giving relevance and intimacy but set up to challenge and bring opposing viewpoints: products built not to give easy answers that weaken human development, but to make us better in the broadest sense.
