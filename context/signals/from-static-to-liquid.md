# From static to liquid

The digital economy has historically been built on information and access to that information. Since the early internet, organisations created reach by producing more content, capturing attention, and securing visibility in search. Generative AI is starting to reorganise that logic.

## From information to context

- In an AI-mediated world, competitive advantage depends less on simply making information available and more on understanding what matters in a given moment, for a given person, in a given situation. The shift is moving from information to context. From static to liquid.
- Liquid content dynamically adapts in real time across formats, platforms, contexts and users rather than existing as one fixed version.
- This is already visible in AI-driven search interfaces such as Perplexity and Google AI Overview, where synthesised answers appear directly inside the interface rather than through links to external websites. If that direction continues, we are moving toward a zero-click ecosystem where you get all you need without having to click.
- Recent developments in so-called vibe coding for building websites, digital products and tools point to a future in which both workflows and products can be iterated and continuously reimagined. In both cases, value lies with the accessibility to the right type of knowledge, context and situational awareness of where it is being applied.

## From attention to intention

- The attention economy begins to give way to the intention economy. Instead of competing mainly for visibility, organisations need to interpret the intent behind an interaction and respond in ways that are relevant, timely and situationally aware.
- Context-aware systems can adapt interfaces, recommendations, support and content in real time. This is already visible in small pockets of future in generative commerce, adaptive assistants and more responsive work tools. Personal relevance, timing and situational fit become strategic assets in their own right.
- The implications go far beyond retail or media. When AI systems become the first point of contact, organisations are no longer competing only for attention in crowded information environments. They are competing for inclusion in the systems that interpret intent, shape visibility, and guide action before a user ever reaches a traditional interface. Value may shift away from the interface that attracts the click and toward the infrastructure, context and trusted service that shape the response itself in the moment.

## Challenges

- The quality of liquid content depends on context, data richness, inference and interpretation, which raises questions around privacy, misreading, manipulation and fairness.
- Tacit knowledge, social cues and cultural understanding remain difficult to capture through data alone.
- As organisations lose some control over static content in a more liquid digital ecosystem, the challenge is not about simply producing more content, since the static 'artifact' of content is no longer where the value lies. It is about finding new ways to stay relevant.

## Across the horizons

This shift could begin with a weaker link between visibility and value, as context, timing and intent become increasingly important. It then moves toward products, services and workflows built around more adaptive and fluid interactions. Over time, value may shift away from static content and clicks, and toward systems shaped on new dimensions of meaning, relevance and trust.

## This includes

- Liquid content
- Context aware AI
- Intent based interfaces
- Generative services and commerce

## Questions

- What new services or business models become possible when interfaces respond to intent rather than traditional search behaviour?
- How do we prepare for a possible zero click ecosystem where AI interfaces intermediate access to information and services?
- What kinds of contextual understanding would create genuine value for our customers, employees, or partners, and what kinds would feel intrusive or manipulative?
- How do we collect and use contextual data responsibly, with clear limits, transparency, and consent?
- What capabilities do we need to design for relevance and adaptability without undermining our values and ethical guidelines?
