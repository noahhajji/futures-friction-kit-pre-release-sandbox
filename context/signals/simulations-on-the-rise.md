# Simulations on the rise

AI is turning simulation into a core preparedness capability, allowing organisations to test futures in playful manners before they must live through them. Many of the challenges organisations face now are complex, uncertain and systemic. Traditional wargaming often struggles to account for second order effects, feedback loops, or the way multiple shifts interact as conditions change.

## A new experimental space

- AI driven simulation creates a new experimental space for foresight, leadership, training, innovation and decision making. It allows organisations to explore plausible scenarios, rehearse responses, and experience how systems, teams, strategies and other implications may behave as different futures begin to unfold.
- One frontier is the use of synthetic data, AI generated datasets, simulated personas and agent populations. Such data allow organisations to test features, service journeys, behavioural responses and alternative future conditions before exposing real customers or sensitive data to emerging products.
- Used well, simulation can turn experimentation into a practical and even playful way for organisations to engage with possible futures. Used inadequately, it can produce false confidence, especially when the modelled world is too neat, too biased, or too far removed from how people actually behave.

## The link between foresight and practice

- Simulation provides a strong link between foresight and any given future practice. When scenario work, digital twins, agent based modelling or synthetic data are grounded in real organisational uncertainties, futures become easier to test.
- Instead of relying on static narratives, organisations can explore how different situations might unfold, where pressure points could emerge, and which decisions hold up across several possible worlds.
- It can also bring in a wider set of voices, giving more people a role in shaping how those futures are explored.

## Across the horizons

AI enhanced simulation may look like a support tool for planning and training today. Over time, it may become an invaluable resource for rehearsing decisions, testing assumptions, exposing hidden dependencies, and building preparedness before reality forces adaptation.

## This includes

- Simulation environments
- Synthetic data
- Digital twins
- AI-driven foresight systems

## Questions

- Which strategic decisions in our organisation would benefit most from simulation before real world execution?
- How can we connect simulation tools with foresight, risk management, and leadership development rather than treating them as separate activities?
- What data, assumptions, and governance are needed to make simulations useful without confusing plausible models with certainty?
- How might simulation change the speed and quality of organisational learning in times of volatility or crisis?
- Where could simulated environments become a core resource for preparedness, experimentation, and strategic communication?
