# Audiences move to creators

Trust in journalism has been challenged over time, but in 2026 it reached a ten-year global low. Changed habits among users, especially in the younger generations, have also long pulled away from established news media towards creators and influencers, with news avoidance as a rising phenomenon. That development has accelerated too. Among the young, sixteen per cent use AI for news.

The young have chosen sides: they have chosen video platforms, not the established media. So one has to come to them, and use forms of communication that reach them.

## What is happening

- News creators and influencers have seen marked growth in recent years, with individual interview formats on news and politics reaching hundreds of thousands of views on average. A key to their success is understanding the forms and formats of communication on social media: taking formats that already work there and translating them to news and politics.
- Authenticity is a new currency, and trust arises not from striven-for objectivity but from competence, integrity and goodwill, meaning close contact with one's community.
- Some established media systematically select already-established young creators and then train them, in ethics and research tools for instance. Creators are audience infrastructure: they bring new users to the medium's other offerings.
- Where trust has no reach, it has failed; if established media do not engage, the public service of the future will be built outside them.

## A regional caveat

News media in Denmark and the Nordic countries stand stronger than almost anywhere else. Young Danes largely back news media, and usage figures are often far better than abroad. That gives a starting point and a surplus to act. But the tendencies seen elsewhere in the West exist here too, and everything suggests they will only grow in strength in the coming years.
