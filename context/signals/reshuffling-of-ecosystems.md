# The reshuffling of ecosystems

One of the clearest signals is an early reshuffle of competitive ecosystems. AI is starting to change where value is created and captured, not only inside organisations, but across the wider system of platforms, ecosystems, interfaces, infrastructure layers, service providers and end users. As AI becomes embedded across these layers, value does not disappear. It moves.

## From the object to the system

- AI is changing the centre of gravity in many industries. In an AI-mediated economy, advantage may shift from owning the thing to shaping the flow around it.
- The strongest position may belong less to the company that makes the product, and increasingly to the one that interprets intent, coordinates data, and becomes the trusted layer through which decisions are made. This is the reshuffle, when value moves from the object to the system around it.
- AI is rearranging relationships between infrastructure providers, model companies, interface owners, data holders, service layers and end users across global value chains. Organisations can no longer assume that their historical place in their market will hold.

## Which skills become valuable

- Demand is rising for capabilities such as AI literacy, context engineering, output verification, and the ability to manage agentic workflows. Communication, interdisciplinary thinking, and the ability to work critically with AI-generated outputs remain essential.
- The deeper capability required for executive decision-makers becomes system literacy: the ability to understand how value is created across new ecosystems, where dependencies are shifting, and how changes in one layer can alter the logic of the whole.
- Future relevance is likely to depend on which of these capabilities organisations build, combine and embed across the workforce, not as isolated crafts but as ways of navigating the system.
- The organisations that understand how the ecosystem is being reconfigured, and can reposition themselves within it, might be the ones that stay relevant. Ecosystem thinking is becoming a core leadership capability.

## Across the horizons

This reshuffle can be easy to miss at first because it often appears as interface improvement, process automation or better tooling. Over time it changes partnerships, power positions, and the basis on which companies compete and stay relevant.

## This includes

- AI platforms
- Data value chains
- Competition between infrastructure layers

## Questions

- Where is value likely to move in our sector as AI changes the relationship between interfaces, data, infrastructure, and end users?
- Are we defending the right part of the value chain, or optimising a layer that may become commoditised?
- What new partners, competitors, or cross industry players should we now consider part of our future strategic landscape?
- How do we build strengths in trust, orchestration, and integration, rather than relying only on current product logic?
- What signals suggest that our ecosystem is being reshuffled faster than our strategy assumes?
