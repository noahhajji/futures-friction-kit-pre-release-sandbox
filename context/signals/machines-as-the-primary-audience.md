# Machines as the primary audience

## The two shifts

- Machines are becoming the primary audience for information. A growing share of what is published, recorded or measured will be read first, and often only, by AI systems acting for people, and increasingly by agents acting for other agents.
- Information changes category. For most of its history, news, information and knowledge have been a final good, produced before consumption and read by a person who may or may not act on it. In an agentic economy they become an intermediate input into a decision, a transaction or an automated process, and in most cases may never be rendered for a human at all.

## Signs

- In October 2026, Meta and Sierra announced a protocol for how personal agents deal with businesses, with Walmart, Shopify and Stripe on board.
- Agent protocols such as MCP and content credentials such as C2PA are early versions of a shared protocol and standard unit for information. Data cooperatives such as Switzerland's MIDATA, whose members decide who may use their health data, are an early version of trusted holders of dispersed knowledge. Probabilistic news, which attaches explicit likelihoods to claims, is an early version of calibrated confidence. Crossref's Crossmark, which flags corrected and retracted research, is an early version of corrections that reach every system that used the original.
- The agentic information economy does not yet have a standard way to represent a piece of information, let alone the institutions to build on one.

## What it signals

- Shuwei Fang's reading: the foundations of the agentic economy are being laid, but the information systems it runs on lag behind. Whether journalism's instincts, independence, disclosure, scrutiny and public correction, become the blueprints is being decided now, while the standards are still soft.
