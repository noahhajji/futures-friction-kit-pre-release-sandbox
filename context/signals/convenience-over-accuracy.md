# Convenience over accuracy

Something suggests that more news users are already getting a different relationship to truth and facts. The majority in two studies were perfectly satisfied with merely approximately correct information from AI, because it was more convenient than classic news media, even though those were more correct. When the whole structure of news media is set up to deliver precisely accurate information, that tendency constitutes a possible existential challenge.

## What it challenges

- The whole structure of news media is set up to deliver precise information, so users who are indifferent to precision remove the premise of that structure.
- It raises the question whether people will pay at all for information being of slightly higher quality, and whether free and convenience are in fact more important. Key people in even well-established media ask themselves the same question.
- Knowledge is never finished or fixed, and often it is simply wrong; AI-generated overviews err in roughly one answer in ten, and the concept of authority is slipping.

## What it calls for

If news media are to succeed, it probably requires pronounced innovation of forms and formats that give users the desire for more truthful information, and show very clearly why it is important. That task falls to news media, and to their clarity about who they are, but also to politicians and others who support a viable press. Developing self-correcting mechanisms that people will actually use or pay for needs many different disciplines: not just tech experts, politicians, financiers and communicators, but philosophers, anthropologists, artists and psychologists.
