# AI moves into the physical world

Robotics is entering a new phase. What has been confined to automated arms in advanced manufacturing is now leaving the warehouses. Humanoid robots, autonomous machines and other embodied robotic systems are beginning to operate in spaces built for people, not just machines. This marks a shift from automation as fixed infrastructure to robotics as a more flexible operational layer in the real world.

## Beyond manufacturing and logistics

- With the entry of humanoids, robots are no longer just relevant in manufacturing or logistics. As sensing, perception, mobility, language interfaces and planning improve, robotic systems might take on tasks in more everyday settings, where labour might be scarce, routines are repetitive, or safety is a concern.
- Robotics is becoming part of a wider restructuring of how work is organised and where value is created.

## Benefits and risks

- Robots may help address labour shortages, improve workplace safety, reduce operational friction, and create new forms of service and support.
- Once robotic systems move from controlled automation to semi-autonomous action in dynamic settings, questions of liability, safety, governance, trust and interoperability become much harder. A software failure in a chatbot is one thing. A robotic failure in a workplace, shop floor, hospital or public setting is something else entirely.

## Across the horizons

Robotics begins by improving specific tasks and isolated functions. It then starts to reshape workflows, maintenance systems, and collaboration between humans and machines across space. In the longer term, it may alter labour structures, service expectations, workplace design, and the competitive importance of physical environments built to work seamlessly with intelligent machines. Robotics should not be viewed only as a hardware story. It is also a story about organisational preparedness, systems design, and the future shape of work.

## This includes

- Humanoid robotics
- Autonomous robots
- Robotic systems in operations
- Physical AI in embodied machines

## Questions

- Where in our organisation could robots create genuine operational value, and where are they still too immature to justify adoption?
- Which tasks are best suited for robotic systems, and which still depend on human judgement, dexterity, trust, or care?
- How do we prepare for safety, liability, and accountability when robots begin acting in environments shared with people?
- What organisational and infrastructural changes are needed to integrate robots into workflows, maintenance, training, and decision systems?
- How might robotics change expectations around labour, service, workplace design, and competitive advantage in our sector?
