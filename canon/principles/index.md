# Principles

- [Strategic foresight can be applied to a broad range of organisational contexts](/canon/principles/broad-range-of-organisational-contexts.md): when someone asks what foresight is good for or whether it fits their purpose, from anticipating risks and innovation to stress-testing strategy or thought leadership.
- [Strategic foresight seeks to challenge mental models and organisational perspectives](/canon/principles/challenge-mental-models.md): when a conversation defaults to the business-as-usual future or an outdated image of it, and the person needs a reason to question the assumptions behind it.
- [Strategic foresight explores the future before considering implications for the present](/canon/principles/explore-the-future-before-the-present.md): when the person wants to start from today's situation or plan, and needs to see why foresight first explores the future and then reads the present through it.
- [Foresight and strategy are complementary parts of one process](/canon/principles/foresight-and-strategy-are-one-process.md): when someone expects foresight to deliver a strategy, or plans strategy without foresight, and needs to see how the two connect.
- [Strategic foresight should be approached as a form of collective intelligence](/canon/principles/foresight-as-collective-intelligence.md): when the person plans to do foresight alone or with a like-minded group and needs to see why diverse perspectives and dialogue are essential.
- [Strategic foresight looks beyond the traditional strategic planning time horizon](/canon/principles/look-beyond-the-planning-horizon.md): when the discussion is confined to the three-to-five-year planning horizon and the person needs a reason to look ten or more years out.
- [Strategic foresight explores plausible futures rather than making predictions](/canon/principles/plausible-futures-not-predictions.md): when someone asks what will happen or wants a forecast, and needs to understand why foresight explores plausible futures instead of predicting.
- [Applying strategic foresight in practice is not an academic exercise](/canon/principles/practice-not-academic-exercise.md): when someone doubts foresight is rigorous, or fears it is too theoretical to use, and needs to see how method and practical judgement combine.
- [The strategic foresight process is at least as important as the final outcomes](/canon/principles/process-matters-as-much-as-outcomes.md): when the person judges foresight only by its outputs and needs to see the value of the process in learning and shared understanding.
- [Strategic foresight is a systems-thinking approach to support resilient organisational futures](/canon/principles/systems-thinking-for-resilient-futures.md): when the person relies on forecasts and numbers alone and needs to see why external change, outcomes beyond the numbers and overlooked signals should shape decisions.
