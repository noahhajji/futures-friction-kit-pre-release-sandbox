# The Ma brief

A simple experiment over a couple of months, built around one continuing issue, is enough to begin exploring journalism with room to move. No agent that understands everything about us is needed first.

## Start with

- One continuing issue: a school merger, an energy transition, a hospital reorganisation, a housing plan.
- One story that can help people understand the issue and, if they choose, do something about it.

## The brief

Before anyone writes a line of code, before the feature list and before the prompt engineering, write one page answering four questions:

1. Where does it end? When does a person know they are done, and how does the product tell them?
2. Where does it pause? Which moves should wait, ask twice, or suggest sleeping on it?
3. What does it open? What will it offer that nobody asked for, chosen by whom, and labelled how?
4. When does it fall silent? What is the threshold below which it says nothing, and how can a person check that the story is still being followed?

## The experiment

- Keep the first version small. Offer a few supported ways to explore or apply the reporting, and make it easy to see how a person's question connects to the wider issue.
- Test it with a small group that includes people who rarely use the newsroom's products. Compare their experience with the article on its own and with the newsroom's existing alerts.
- Ask: did they understand the issue more accurately? Did they encounter a relevant question they had not considered? Could they take a useful step on their own terms? Could they finish when they had what they needed, without feeling pushed to keep interacting or to take a suggested action?
- Record the failures and the maintenance costs as carefully as the successful uses, then share the findings publicly so other newsrooms can learn what helped, what failed and what it took to maintain.

## The closing question

After spending time with this journalism, do you have more room to think and act for yourself?

## Key things to consider

- A calmer interface does not make reporting cheap. An acting story creates new costs as well as new value, and the people who would benefit most from help navigating public decisions may have the least money to spend on it.
- By 2030 this could start with a few stories that offer a useful service and grow only if the benefit is worth the work of maintaining them.
