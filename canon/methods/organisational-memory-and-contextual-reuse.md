# Organisational memory and contextual reuse

Bring an earlier attempt into a current decision together with the conditions it was tried under, so that its lesson is tested rather than repeated blindly or refused on memory.

## When it helps

- Someone says "we tried something like that and it didn't work".
- Nobody knows why an earlier attempt was shelved; the person who ran it has left.
- An idea is about to be built that someone in the organisation has probably built before.

## The assumption to test

"We tried that" settles the matter only if what stopped the last attempt was a property of the idea rather than of the year it was tried in. A fresh attempt is safe only if none of those blockers is still standing.

## How it works

1. Find the last attempt's owner, its evaluation or its deck. Write down what it set out to do, what it showed, and what stopped it: the technology was too complicated, no one owned it, there was no time, the moment was wrong.
2. Put two columns on the whiteboard, headed with the year of the earlier attempt and this year, and take the attempt through Enablers and blockers as if it were a trend. In the first column, list what carried the attempt as far as it got and what stopped it. In the second, mark each of those forces stronger, weaker or gone, and add any new blocker that did not exist then.
3. Read the result. If the stopping forces have weakened, the idea has an inflection point and this year may be it. If they stand where they stood, the new attempt is the old one under a new name.
4. Keep the earlier result next to the new reading, with who ran it and when, and log the answer where the next person who has the idea will find it. Where no record exists at all, ask the longest-serving colleagues, and let the note become the first entry in the record.

## Key things to consider

- The person leaves knowing which forces to watch rather than which idea to avoid.
- A remembered conclusion is one person's account of what happened. The original evaluation may show something narrower, for example that the need was real but the way people had to reach it was not.
- The organisation's memory of an attempt often lives in someone who may have left, so every repeat pays the full cost of discovery again.
- An attempt can fail on timing alone: too early or too late, technologically possible but organisationally immature, or right for a future shift but impossible to anchor at the time.
