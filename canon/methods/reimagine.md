# Reimagine

Reconsider how a product, service or process creates value when its present form is no longer taken for granted.

## When it helps

- Incremental improvement keeps returning the team to the same design.
- A future condition changes who needs the service, how it is encountered or what creates trust.

## Start with

- An identifiable product, service or process.
- An underlying need or purpose to examine.
- A future condition selected by the user. Choose a year only when it helps the inquiry.

## Working with the method

- Make the future condition tangible through a person's day: who they are, what they need, when they encounter information and in which situations. Use relevant moments rather than a compulsory daily timetable.
- Explore the underlying need, inherited assumptions and commitments through first principles and selective Five Whys. Distinguish what must remain true from what can disappear, including the current format or role.
- Rebuild the configuration: who interacts, including people, agents or both; where interaction happens; how provenance and editorial authority appear; and what role the newsroom plays. Let these dimensions inform one another.
- Use a metaphor to investigate the alternative logic and translate it into operations. In Inayatullah's Causal Layered Analysis, myth and metaphor relate to worldview, social causes and visible accounts of a problem. A metaphor exercise alone is not a full Causal Layered Analysis. An optional, explicitly imagined future headline can describe the surrounding world.
- Examine dependencies and challenges. Three Horizons can clarify whether the configuration changes system logic. Megatrend analysis can test the contextual assumptions; retain promising directions for appropriate testing, including nexus testing once its guidance is available.

## Useful questions

- "What would you build to serve this need under the conditions you have described?"
- "If the service is now a living memory, what must change in its ownership, corrections and everyday use?"

## Capture

- Object.
- Need.
- Future condition.
- Person and situation.
- Commitments.
- Assumptions.
- Who, where, trust and newsroom role.
- Metaphor or imagined headline, if used.
- Operational meaning.
- Dependencies.

## Worked example

A constructed illustration, not an observed case.

- During an imagined evening journey, a resident asks how a council proposal has changed.
- A newsroom reimagines its archive from "warehouse of published articles" to "public memory".
- The concept links claims across time, shows sources and corrections, and lets a journalist review contested answers.
- The metaphor exposes a governance question: whose experiences enter that memory and who can challenge omissions?
- The concept remains conditional, not a forecast.

## Quality boundaries

- A distant year, new interface or AI component does not establish transformation.
- Keep the need open to revision. Test what a metaphor hides as well as reveals.
- Day-in-the-life narratives and imagined headlines are constructions, not user research or forecasts.
- The assistant helps articulate the user's configuration without silently choosing their preferred future.

## Exercise sheet: What else could this become?

One of the kit's optional exercises: a one-page canvas with short instructions and space to work, for use with pen, paper and conversation, or alongside the assistant.

- Start with: one news product to rethink and one or two future conditions to explore, drawn from a megatrend or signal.
- The canvas: a day-in-the-life timeline, the product and the need, five whys, an assumption challenger with what must still be true and what can disappear or be transformed, an imagined news headline, and a rebuild for the future year.
- At the end: a reimagined news product for a future year you choose, grounded in a human need and an imagined day.
