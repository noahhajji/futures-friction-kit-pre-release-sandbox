# Enablers and blockers

The way future change occurs depends on the outcome of the power struggle between enabling and limiting forces. Forecasting methods typically derive trends from past data and extrapolate these trends forward without much consideration of the forces that nurture the trend and may eventually alter it. By using the Enablers and Blockers framework, we can get a better understanding of the dynamics of change of a given trend development.

## How it works

- This understanding is gained by identifying underlying dynamics in favouring forces (enablers) that create, sustain and catalyse a trend, and limiting forces (blockers) that stand in the way of a trend and slow it down, possibly even diverting it.
- In essence, this provides a framework for analysing factors that will influence and define how a specific development plays out.
- Understanding a trend or development as a reflection of underlying enabling and blocking forces reveals why trend trajectories are capable of sudden surprises and reversals.
- When thinking about future change, we always have to keep in mind that while a lot of things might change significantly, many things might also stay the same.

## Use enablers and blockers to

- Understand a trend as a reflection of underlying enabling and blocking forces.
- Acknowledge the complexity inherent in how change occurs.
- Better anticipate the inflection points where a trend's trajectory might shift.

## Key things to consider

- Start by assessing how a given trend is currently impacting your area of interest (industry, sector, etc.). This is followed by brainstorming factors that promote or catalyse the trend (enablers) and the factors that stop, limit or divert the trend (blockers).
- Try to construct your own trend development trajectory that reflects the power struggle between the identified enablers and blockers. Where/when enablers are strong and blockers are weak, we can expect the pace of change to be rapid. Where blockers are strong, we should expect them to deflect or slow the trend. Finally, discuss the future impact and implications that derive from the development.
- Be aware that sociocultural factors are often a key source of trend blocking. If the trend or development challenges prevailing social, cultural or ethical norms, it is likely that change will only proceed slowly, if at all.
- Equally important to identifying enablers and blockers is to identify potential trend breaks (a moment that can signal the end of an existing trend and the start of a new one) and inflection points (a moment where the rate of change shifts significantly, often marking a transition in the growth or decline phase of a trend). This can have wide-ranging implications for current strategic plans and priorities. Very often it is about timing.

## The friction field

- New trends and developments create friction in the existing environment, and the future unfolds in that friction field. Change enters on one side; implications for societies, industries and the business environment emerge on the other. Above the field act the turners and blockers, below it the drivers and enablers.
- A change driver may itself be a trend. Change blockers are the forces that oppose a trend by delaying, stopping or diverting it, and the factors that cause resistance to change.
- Whose side is the law on? The law is almost always, by design, a drag on the future, for good and for bad.
- Do not take a trend at face value. A trend can relatively easily be identified and analysed, but understanding the forces that promote or limit it gives the more detailed picture, and very often it is about timing.
