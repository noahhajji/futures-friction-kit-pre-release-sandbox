# Assumptions

Assumptions are the underlying beliefs and expectations that shape how we make decisions and anticipate change. They are what we take to be true, or likely to unfold in a certain way, often without being explicitly tested or questioned.

## Two kinds

- In decision-making: beliefs, expectations or perceived constraints taken as given when making choices and setting priorities.
- In forecasting: foundational premises about future conditions that guide models or predictions.

## Why they matter for foresight

- The worst assumptions are sometimes the ones left unchallenged.
- Assumptions about the future are mostly rooted in the present and in a path-dependent view of the future, often based on beliefs never thought about critically. A critical futures thinker is attentive to them because they are sometimes incorrect or misguided, frozen in time as the world has shifted around them.
- Foresight exists to question the entrenched and misguided assumptions that underpin an understanding of the future that is rarely tested or questioned explicitly, and that so remains disconnected from emerging realities.
- Everyone involved in a foresight process brings their own beliefs and assumptions about what the future holds, consciously and subconsciously, and these influence planning, decisions and actions.
