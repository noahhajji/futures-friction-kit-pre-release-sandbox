# The AI-mediated framework

AI increasingly surrounds us as human beings. Work, information, consumption and communication are shaped by systems that filter signals, interpret context and recommend actions. In short, we become mediated by AI. CIFS designed the AI-mediated framework to illustrate this shift. It describes how humans operate within today's interconnected layers, ranging from contextual AI and hardware to foundational infrastructure and worldview. Each layer shapes the next, creating a system with AI in a constant loop.

## When mediation becomes agentic

- The 'intelligence' surrounding humans is not only becoming more contextual and embedded, but also agentic. AI agents can retrieve information, coordinate actions, and interact with other systems across their own digital systems without iterative human input.
- The framework introduces a new dynamic where AI agents begin to interact with each other directly, forming emerging agentic loops that circulate information, decisions and actions across the layers, without always requiring a human in the loop. AI is no longer only at everyone's fingertips; it is also beginning to interact in its own closed system.

## The machine-readable layer

- Alongside the internet designed primarily for human communication, a machine-readable layer is emerging where information is structured so AI systems can interpret and act upon it.
- The human-facing internet remains the landscape we know, where we publish stories, create content, design interfaces and communicate in formats created for people. At the same time a parallel environment is forming where value increasingly depends on structured data, interoperable systems and machine-readable metadata that AI systems and agents can interpret.
- Content is therefore not only written to be read by humans, but also structured to be processed, recombined and acted upon by machines.

## Augmentation and mediation

- Augmentation focuses on the individual and describes how AI enhances human capabilities by helping people analyse information, automate tasks, or generate new forms of content.
- Mediation focuses on the broader system and describes how intelligent technologies shape how information flows, how decisions are structured, and how people interact with organisations, markets and each other.
- In practice both dynamics unfold at the same time. AI augments human capabilities while simultaneously mediating the structures in which those capabilities are exercised. The result is not only more capable individuals, but organisations and societies operating within increasingly AI-mediated environments.

## The individual at the centre

- Humans move across physical, digital and quasi realms where identities, roles and participation become more fluid, being present many places at once, and being exposed to new ways of interacting with content through (several) agents, while navigating new challenges such as cognitive fatigue and growing reliance on algorithmic systems.
- Each layer of the framework both enables and constrains the others. Infrastructure shapes what can be built, technologies shape the experiences that emerge, experiences influence behaviour and expectations, and behaviour feeds back into governance, norms and societal narratives. When these layers are evolving simultaneously, change becomes systemic rather than incremental.

## Mediation seen from the newsroom

- Nikita Roy reads the same shift for news: AI is becoming the system that controls how news flows. Quoted by Mosbech, she puts it as whoever controls the aggregation of information has the power, and no news medium has that control; the result can be their gradually disappearing relevance.
- When the AI interface answers, the journalism may be seen while the brand is not, and the interface develops an editorial voice of its own, which is not the newsroom's.
- A business model built on volume cannot survive when generative AI produces effectively infinite text.
- The response Roy argues for is a rebuild around the unique value a newsroom provides rather than its legacy forms, from a content factory to a knowledge and community institution. Mosbech puts the same choice as a question: whether news media are to be content factories, or can build a new trusted layer, interfaces that people will use, also as an alternative to AI products and platforms.

## Figure: the AI-mediated framework with an agentic loop

A set of concentric rings with a sphere at the centre. From the outermost ring inward: worldview (narratives, cultural norms and values, ethics and governance; for example regulation and ethical frameworks guiding technological development), foundational infrastructure (scalable systems enabling everything in the inner layers; for example data infrastructures and simulation environments), experience-enabling technologies (physical technologies bridging digital and physical worlds; for example immersive interfaces, humanoids and physical AI), AI-mediated intelligence (AI systems personalising and adapting interactions; for example context awareness and agentic systems), and at the centre the augmented human (liquid identity, multiple representations, participatory; distributed presence across physical, digital and immersive environments). A line loops out from the ring structure and encircles a second, smaller copy of the same rings with an AI agent at its centre instead of the human. This is the agentic loop: AI agents interacting within their own system, influencing the AI-mediated layers surrounding the human. The model was developed by Sofie Hvitved and Bugge Holm Hansen, Copenhagen Institute for Futures Studies, 2025.
