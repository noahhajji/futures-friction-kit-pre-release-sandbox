# Three publisher positions

The transition to the AI-driven information ecology erodes the publisher's historical control of news production and versioning and moves the locus of use from articles read on websites to multiple uses in AI services. Kasper Lindskow's three positions are the ways a publisher can convert its existing capabilities into control of the new scarcities in the value chain: original trustworthy knowledge, and knowledge of users' intentions and contexts. The journalistic point of departure, trustworthiness to individuals, companies and communities, is the strength shared by all three.

## Originator of scarce information, for agents

- The publisher continues to provide journalism as events unfold, focusing on the news that does not already exist in the ecology and requires human journalistic action and supervision: physical or emotional access to sources and situations, increasingly AI-assisted.
- Value proposition: unique news needed to know the world as it evolves. Strength: unique and trustworthy information.
- Challenge: can scarcity be controlled against other AI-powered originators and the commodification of news content? It calls for defending scarcity through control over access, rights enforcement and uniqueness of reporting, and possibly alliances or consolidation at this step of the chain.

## Knowledge provider, for businesses and institutions

- The publisher departs from producing and publishing news to providing access to its accumulated journalistic knowledge, so that companies and institutions can get the answers they need to do their work. This requires a knowledge base on top of news production, structured to be maintainable, verifiable and retrievable; customers are those who need factual knowledge about the domains the publisher continuously covers.
- Value proposition: all information relevant to a professional intention and context. Strength: accumulated proprietary knowledge.
- Challenge: competition from specialised information services, met by protecting the exclusivity of significant parts of the knowledge base through uniqueness, continuity and trustworthiness, and possibly by extending collection beyond reporting to complementary information.

## News experience provider, for consumers

- The publisher keeps its direct relationship with the broad audience and provides an intelligent interface through which users experience and explore news and apply journalistic knowledge to their interests and needs, making the content more personally valuable through interaction, contextualisation and assistance.
- Value proposition: discover and explore news aligned with your intention and context, as an individual or a member of a community. Strength: the existing relationship with users, still strong, especially in the Nordic countries.
- Challenge: competition from general AI services, met by a differentiated experience focused on news: editorial judgement, exclusive trustworthy knowledge, specialised AI-mediated experiences that prioritise what is editorially important, and a shared experience that shows what the community cares about and enables deliberation.

## The vertically integrated knowledge publisher

- The positions are distinct but not mutually exclusive. Larger publishers could combine all three, addressing agents, businesses and consumers from the same journalistic production and knowledge infrastructure: the ABC model.
- Journalistic knowledge production, reporting, investigation, verification, interpretation and editorial judgement, all highly assisted by AI, feeds a shared journalistic knowledge infrastructure of structured knowledge, evidence, source data, versioning, semantic search and governance. From there it flows to three markets: licensed to external AI services for grounding and verification; served as domain-specific knowledge to companies; experienced in publisher-owned AI products.
- The synergies from economies of scope might be essential for recovering the still high costs of original reporting, enabling investments that distributing to one channel could not sustain. Entering three markets means managing channel conflicts by differentiating the products, resources and rights offered through each.

## Key things to consider

- The model does not change the core mission; it resituates it. When information is continuously updated and reused, publishers must develop methods that ensure accuracy and provenance both at publication and over time.
- When knowledge is transformed to address individual intentions, AI-powered experiences must balance what the user wants and what is journalistically important.
- As AI at scale becomes part of news origination itself, new ways are needed to ensure editorial judgement and emotional connection when humans steer at higher levels of abstraction. These inventions could be essential sources of differentiation.
