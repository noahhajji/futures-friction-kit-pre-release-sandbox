# Three information ecologies

The change AI brings to news publishing is usually perceived as destruction: declining direct traffic, misinformation, job losses, eroding trust. Kasper Lindskow's question, after Schumpeter, is what AI is creating. His answer is a reorganisation of how information is produced, maintained, discovered, interpreted and used, which will gradually displace the ecology of articles, websites and apps with an ecology of agents and flows. The central strategic challenge is then not how to produce news more efficiently but how to redefine the publisher's editorial and economic role when static articles cease to be the main unit of exchange and AI systems mediate between information and its users. The vision is an ambition, not a prediction.

## The three ecologies

- Analogue, before 2000: print, radio and television; industrialised production and distribution; people read, watch and listen; publishers, broadcasters and distributors are the intermediaries; content, distribution capacity and reach are the resources; the publisher is gatekeeper and distributor to mass audiences.
- Digital, 2000 to 2020: internet, search engines, social platforms and smartphones; networked access and algorithmic discovery; people search, browse, navigate and share; search engines, social platforms and app ecosystems are the intermediaries; attention, behavioural data, network effects and audience relationships are the resources; the publisher produces and curates news in a platform-mediated environment and competes for attention.
- AI-driven, from the 2020s: foundation models, AI assistants and agents; machine-mediated interpretation, synthesis, reasoning and action; people question, converse, collaborate and delegate; model providers, AI assistants and agent platforms are the intermediaries; trustworthy knowledge, proprietary data, cognitive capabilities and task completion are the resources; the publisher produces, verifies, maintains and supplies trusted journalistic knowledge to human audiences and AI-mediated systems.

## The shift in the unit of information

- The basic unit may shift from finished media products and static digital objects towards more fundamental units of knowledge that can be recombined to serve different purposes on demand. This separates the production of information from its uses: the same information can flow into numerous services, creating value adapted to the intentions and contexts of individual users.
- The AI-driven ecology now exists in parallel to the digital one and partly builds on it; in the coming years it grows relative to it and eventually becomes the primary mode for information to flow.

## The two-way value chain

Four steps, with knowledge flowing downstream and demand flowing upstream:

1. Origination and validation: new relevant information is produced as real-world events unfold, by machines through sensors and by humans empowered by AI, since some production still requires physical presence and access to sources and situations.
2. Knowledge infrastructure: the original information is organised in an infrastructure holding the accumulated information produced over time. In a news context this can be a database of the facts about the world in the publisher's news flow, relationships between entities and events tied to their source and validity, continuously maintained as facts change, highly automated with human supervision for factuality.
3. Orchestration: information from the infrastructure is orchestrated to address specific needs, by AI systems operating within the guidelines and guardrails of whoever controls them.
4. Relationship: the orchestrated information is presented in AI services that provide answers, understanding, advice, decisions, completed tasks or actions. Upstream, signals about users' information needs inform the orchestration and provide data on what knowledge is in demand.

## The economics

- AI expands information markets by reducing the cost of producing and transforming information and increasing the purposes it can serve.
- AI creates new scarcities. Information that anyone can produce with AI becomes abundant and commoditised; two things stay scarce: original, trustworthy knowledge that AI systems cannot independently produce, and access to the intentions and context of users.
- AI shifts bargaining power towards the organisations that control those scarcities, which are likely to capture a large portion of the value created.
- It is not given that publishers will appropriate more value than today. Creative destruction can create temporary or permanent imbalances between those who create value and those who capture it; monopolies of the leaders of a transformation can become permanent through scale economies, network effects and switching costs, which may call for intervention in the market.
