# Strategic foresight fallacies

Flawed ways of reasoning about, or applying, strategic foresight, drawn from CIFS' own experience.

- Engaging with foresight to look for certainty in uncertain times, rather than using it to challenge misguided assumptions that may underpin a false sense of certainty. Authentic strategic foresight serves the opposite purpose.
- Trying to 'force fit' foresight into current strategic practices and the familiarity of existing routines, rather than using it to balance strategic thinking and broaden the strategic perspective.
- Collapsing foresight into operational planning, reducing it to a planning tool for short-term business intelligence or tactical purposes. This narrows its scope and keeps it stuck in the present, when it should transcend immediate priorities and explore longer-term shifts.
- Misunderstanding foresight's epistemic role in decision-making: expecting it to deliver clear, actionable strategy in and of itself, instead of approaching it as one source of insights that widens the strategic perspective. This leads to foresight being dismissed as 'interesting but not useful'.
- Equating the activity of doing foresight with action, while foresight only has strategic value when it informs and shapes concrete decisions and actions. This often happens because the strategic or decision-making purpose of the exercise was unclear from the outset.
