# Embedding foresight in the organisation

Building confidence in working strategically with the future is not easy. There will be obstacles, and sometimes frustration: resistance to change, inertia, lack of buy-in and even red tape that gets in the way. Foresight as an organisational activity needs room to grow and time to take root, and since it is about making changes to be better prepared for the future, inertia and resistance are its enemies. It always seems there is something more urgent. You cannot talk about corporate foresight without also talking about culture, and culture beats foresight.

## Why leaders resist

- When key stakeholders understand the value of foresight they are supportive and act as a catalyst for the organisation's foresight work. When they do not buy in, they create one of the most significant barriers to effective implementation.
- Foresight may not align with their short-term incentive structures or performance goals.
- Foresight requires embracing uncertainty and ambiguity, which is challenging for leaders who prefer a predictable and controlled environment.
- In organisations that have been successful in the past, official and unofficial ways of doing things get in the way.

## The natural cycle of change

Change is expected to follow a smooth curve from the status quo to a better state. The natural cycle instead dips: the status quo, then disruption met with shock and denial, then anger and fear, before exploration brings acceptance and rebuilding brings commitment.

| Stage | Status quo | Disruption | Exploration | Rebuilding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reaction | Shock, denial | Anger, fear | Acceptance | Commitment |

## Making foresight more urgent

- Not all strategic projects must be foresight projects, but most would benefit from foresight elements.
- Align foresight efforts with the broader organisational effort. Establish a natural connection between foresight and existing ways of working and work priorities; this makes it easier for people to overcome barriers to engaging.
- Promote simple habits around engaging with foresight. For most people foresight appears complex and time-consuming in a fast-paced environment with obvious day-to-day priorities. Small steps can make a big difference, and micro habits, small, simple, easy-to-implement daily or weekly behaviours, can be very powerful: start small, be consistent, and choose one or two that the team can realistically commit to.
- Prioritise carving out space and time for the future. Bringing people out of their day-to-day context helps them break out of routines and be open to prioritising important but less urgent work. Sometimes it is simply a matter of making time and space, both mentally and physically.
