# The CIFS foresight process

CIFS' approach to applied strategic foresight is a rigorous process around understanding what drives change, making sense of the uncertainties that matter most, and transitioning into strategic decision-making. Much work in the futures field is strong on visioning, scanning, mapping, forecasting and planning but weak on execution, action and operation; the later phases deserve the attention.

## Six phases

1. Scope: setting the parameters.
2. Awareness of change and 
3. Dynamics of change and uncertainty: explore dynamics of change and understand high-impact uncertainties.
4. Alternative futures and 
5. Strategic insights and implications: build scenarios, engage with alternative futures and understand implications.
6. Action: transition from foresight to strategic insights; tuning and rethinking strategy.

The process runs as a double diamond. It diverges across awareness of change, converges to a point at the boundary with dynamics of change and uncertainty, diverges again across alternative futures, and converges through strategic insights and action. Assessing critical uncertainties is a deductive phase that follows the exploratory one; the scenario axes are the scaffolding for the second divergence.

## The exploratory, future-back process

The same arc seen from the strategy side:

- A. Gathering insights about the future and understanding dynamics of change.
- B. Understanding strategic uncertainties and certainties.
- C. Developing alternative futures.
- D. Assessing future strategic implications, and/or
- E. Stress-testing the viability of current strategic elements.
- F. Future-back to widen strategic perspective and shape strategic decisions.

## Key things to consider

- Every futures project is different. Some are large scale, with extensive scenario development, detailed research and stakeholder workshops to identify and advise on future strategic challenges. Others are small scale, perhaps a single workshop with an internal strategy team to explore what is driving change. The tools are adaptable and can be customised to most futures projects.
- Scoping matters. Sometimes a scoping workshop precedes the process, when the client is not sharp on the scope.
- In a real project, the explore phase is a mix of trend research and curation of second- and third-tier things, interviews with experts, and workshops with the client. It is probably the most cumbersome component, and the understanding and sense-making a good futures thinker brings remain key.
