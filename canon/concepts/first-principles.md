# First principles

First Principles is an approach to fundamental, or existential, innovation. It comes from ancient Greece and consists of peeling a thing down to a basic assumption that cannot be reduced further: "the first basis from which a thing is known", in Aristotle's definition. Since then it has proved one of the most effective ways to solve complicated problems and create fundamental innovation, from Gutenberg's printing press to Musk's rockets. Taking a problem apart into its smallest parts makes it far easier to make new combinations, or other changes, that create fundamental innovation.

## The questions

- What are we completely sure is true or right?
- Ask why five times of every challenge you face, as Nikita Roy puts it.
- If we started today without the history of the newspaper, the news medium or journalism, what would we build to solve users' needs?

## Why it matters now

- AI holds enormous possibilities for developing entirely new ways of being a medium. To realise them so that the result stands out and matches the medium's mission, the core value assumptions must be in place first.
- The approach can lead to the existential innovations that create the journalism or news media of the future, whatever they come to be called. If news media do not create them, others will, probably the tech giants, and that is already happening.
- Seen from the newsroom, Roy's argument runs: when the infrastructure shifts, industries must return to first principles and re-engineer themselves to meet new realities, examining what value only their newsroom can create. The challenge is rebuilding around the unique value a newsroom provides, not around legacy forms built for another era. A business model built on volume cannot survive when generative AI produces effectively infinite text. The rebuild moves the newsroom from a content factory to a knowledge and community institution, and from production-heavy workflows to always-on knowledge environments. Her account is written as a prediction for 2026.

## Letting things fall

- An important part of the approach is the will to let things fall. Perhaps today's way of publishing is not what best realises the basic value assumption. Perhaps success should be measured quite differently; page views as a metric are already strongly challenged in many places. Perhaps the output should not be articles at all, but another format. Something must die for the new to grow.
- Innovation is often doing less. It leads back to the value of cutting away and focusing; often the task is not to add something but to remove something.

## Examples

Media that have taken the approach, in Mosbech's account: Patch's hyperlocal AI newsletters, published for every local area as long as a single user wants one; a one-person cycling-sport medium that rethought entirely how that journalism is made and spread; and VG X, a publication that thinks the Norwegian VG through from scratch, as if the paper were made today with AI and technologists as its load-bearing pillars, published as its own app alongside the ordinary VG, with the fear of cannibalising the main medium set aside.

## Key things to consider

- The approach belongs with knowing yourself. Clarity about the core, who the medium is and why it exists, is what allows choices and omissions, and a clear core vision is typically not about formats, distribution or business models, which is where innovation can then unfold.
- First Principles is the broader approach; Five Whys is one technique within it.
