# Scenarios

Plausible and coherent narratives describing alternative futures, based on key drivers and uncertainties, and expanded through storytelling to create credible and compelling images of how the future may unfold.

## Scenarios exist in sets

- In foresight, scenarios often exist in sets, allowing different futures to be contrasted with one another and with the present, helping to break away from a deterministic view of the future.

## Scenarios are not predictions

- They are not meant to be 'right' or 'wrong', 'good' or 'bad'.
- Their function is to challenge current assumptions and provoke us to think about the future in new ways, including outcomes 'beyond the numbers' that might otherwise be overlooked.

## Scenario planning

- The discipline of scenario planning offers a structured approach to exploring a set of plausible future conditions that are different from the present.
- This allows organisations to widen their strategic perspective and consider alternative future outcomes that may be difficult to grasp in the present.

## What scenarios are

- Compelling, plausible narratives about plausible futures, to facilitate a futures-oriented conversation and understanding.
- Focused on potential changes in the external environment, which in turn influence the strategic environment and strategic decisions.
- Guides that inform organisations and people about future threats and opportunities, to better plan for the future.
- A way to foresee potential outcomes 'beyond the numbers', in an era where forecasts and projections are not enough.

## What scenarios are not

- Not predictions of the future, and not futures to which probabilities should be assigned.
- Not focused on operational options created from an inside-out perspective.
- Not strategies in and of themselves, though they provide insight for planning.
- Not the same as trend analysis, empirical forecasting or other foresight methods.
