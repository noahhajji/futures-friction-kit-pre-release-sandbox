# Liquid foresight

Foresight is fundamentally not about predicting the 'one' future. It is about talking about futures in plural, creating directional awareness, improving the quality of strategic questions, and in doing so providing organisations with the confidence to move between plausible futures, scenarios and horizons with more clarity and less false certainty. But what if foresight were designed for a world mediated by AI?

## What it is

- Liquid foresight, a term coined at the CIFS H3 AI Lab, is an AI-mediated foresight approach that rethinks how foresight itself can evolve towards the third horizon. The aim is a more adaptive practice, one that connects long-term perspectives and core foresight principles with dynamic, real-time signals and contextual shifts personalised to the individual end users.
- Rather than seeing foresight primarily as a static product or a time-limited exercise, it becomes a more continuous and adaptive layer inside the organisation's AI systems. A constantly evolving and adapting process. An ongoing interface between possible futures and present decisions.

## A more dynamic flow of information

- Liquid foresight draws inspiration from liquid content and the possibility of making futures knowledge more interactive, more multimodal, and more responsive to a context, a role and a situation.
- Different parts of an organisation need different things from foresight. A board may need strategic direction. An innovation team may need inspiration for ideas to test. A product team may need an understanding of changing user expectations translated into something they can act on right away.
- The ambition is not necessarily one foresight output for everyone, but a more fluid system that stays anchored in shared direction while adapting to context.

## The Horizon 3 perspective

- Horizon 3 is not only about imagining distant futures. It is about connecting it to the assumptions built into today's ways of working.
- If foresight were invented today, in a world already shaped by AI, simulations, agents, constant flow of insights and machine-readable knowledge, it might not take the form inherited from earlier strategic practice. It might look more like a living knowledge infrastructure, built around iterative loops between the assumptions we test and the contextual data through which we explore multiple futures. A system that connects signals, assumptions, methods, scenarios and decisions across time. Something closer to a self-iterating context graph than a static playbook. Something that can support re-perception not once, but continuously.

## The core principles remain

- Liquid foresight is not about replacing the core principles of foresight. It is about asking how those principles might evolve in an AI-mediated world.
- The need for humans to challenge their own assumptions, broaden their outlook, and work with uncertainty in genuinely participatory processes remains essential. However, the form foresight takes may change. Instead of ending when a scenario is delivered, it might continue through feedback loops, simulations, updated signals, and role-specific interfaces that keep futures thinking alive inside the organisation. Foresight can become more hyper-personalised, multimodal and responsive, while still anchored in a shared strategic direction.
- The core of liquid foresight is adaptive sensemaking, where signals are clustered, challenged, connected, and translated into patterns, tensions and possible implications. AI can extend the capacity of foresight by helping scan information flows, surface inconsistencies, work with context, generate alternative framings, and support simulation.
- The point is not to move away from the process of re-perception and adapting futures thinking. It is to make it more alive, more usable, and more connected to action in the organisational environment for which it was designed. Fundamentally, liquid foresight stresses a shift from sprinkling a little 'AI-salt' on existing foresight processes to building AI-native ways for people and organisations to imagine, work with, and shape futures and scenarios.

## Key principles of liquid foresight

1. From a deliverable to a dynamic capability: foresight that functions as a living capability within the organisation rather than a static output.
2. From a fixed view to live sensemaking: it should connect long-term thinking with signals, tensions, simulations and contextual shifts as they emerge and are relevant.
3. From one-size-fits-all to adaptive intelligence: different roles need different foresight interfaces, but all should remain aligned to a shared strategic direction.

## How to experiment now with AI-integrated, liquid foresight

1. Explore Horizon 3 futures with AI. Use AI to explore radically different futures in which intelligent systems reshape organisations, markets, and the foundations of value creation and competitiveness in the third horizon.
2. Prototype AI-native foresight tools. Test simulations, agent-based models and structured knowledge systems that help explore the futures.
3. Use AI to challenge assumptions. Let it act as a provocation partner, not just an assistant. Under human oversight, use its unexpected outputs to open up new questions and alternative ways of seeing.
4. Work with liquid content rather than static outputs. Build foresight as a dynamic knowledge layer that can evolve continuously and adapt to different users, contexts and decisions.
