# Emerging technologies within a wider nexus

The future of technology has never been something that can be understood through technology alone. Technological change unfolds within a world already undergoing deep, perennial societal reorganisation. Understanding the future of technology requires recognising how technological systems intersect with other structural forces like demographic change, hybrid lifestyles, work, reconfigured sovereignty, a fragmenting geopolitical landscape and shifting structures of trust.

## Interaction between shifts

- Strategic implications come from the interaction between multiple shifts unfolding at the same time, across technology, geopolitics, infrastructure, societal developments, workforce, climate and human behaviour.
- When these forces begin to reinforce one another in a complex nexus, they most profoundly change the conditions under which organisations operate, as technological and societal shifts converge to reshape value creation and competitive advantage across the economy.

## The forces in the nexus

- Lifestyles are more fluid and uneven. Work, learning and everyday life now occur within both physical and digital spaces, but access to the conditions that enable this flexibility is far from equally distributed. Not everybody can participate across societal and cultural layers.
- Demographic changes add further strain, as ageing populations in some regions and expanding youth populations in others reshape labour markets and economic expectations.
- The nature of trust may be shifting. Trust is becoming more conditional and context-specific, as people appear to rely less on formal institutions alone and more on interpersonal networks, local community members, and hyper-personalised digital communities to make sense of the world.
- The world order is becoming more fragmented, and the geopolitical landscape affects us to a greater degree. Authorities are contested, dependencies are more political, and private digital infrastructures once treated as neutral, such as cloud storage, payment systems and data centres, are now bound up with questions of sovereignty, standards and control.

## Why foresight

- Strategic foresight allows technological futures to be considered within a complex nexus of forces driving change, working from the possible futures back to today's decisions, rather than only extending current assumptions forward.
