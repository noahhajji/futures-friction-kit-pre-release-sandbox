# The three horizons of AI-mediated transformation

At first, many organisations approach AI as a means of improving what already exists, making tasks quicker, reducing friction and reinforcing current operations, looking for a quick return on investment and proof of concept of what AI can do. This is justified. Yet the deeper transformation is not only about augmentation at the level of the individual. It unfolds across workflows, organisational systems, and the wider competitive ecosystems in which organisations compete. This perspective echoes the reshuffling of the knowledge economy described by Sangeet Paul Choudary, which explores how AI does not simply automate tasks but reorganises how work, decision-making and value creation are structured across organisations and ecosystems.

## The three horizons

- In strategic foresight, the three horizons framing helps distinguish between the system that currently dominates (H1), the transitional developments that respond to its decline (H2), and the longer-term configuration beginning to appear at the edges of the present (H3).
- In the first horizon, AI helps optimise what already exists. In the second, it begins to reorganise how work flows. In the third, it reshapes business models, competitive boundaries, and the broader systems of value creation.
- Organisations need to work across all three horizons at the same time, else they risk being obsolete when the third horizon occurs.

## The strategic risk

- A company can become highly effective in Horizon 1 while underinvesting in Horizon 2 and remaining blind to Horizon 3. It may automate current processes brilliantly while missing the fact that the ecosystem around it is being redrawn.
- An auditor may become highly skilled at using advanced AI assistants in their analyses, but if the auditing firm fails to prepare for a world of agentic auditors, the firm may lose its competitive edge.
- AI transformation must be understood as more than marginal efficiency improvements at the level of the employee. It is a question of organisational design, strategic timing and long-term relevance.

## Figure: the three horizons of AI-mediated transformation

A three-horizons line chart plotted against time. H1 starts high and declines steadily. H2 starts low, rises to a peak in the middle, then declines. H3 starts lowest, rises steadily and ends highest. The model is inspired in part by the reshuffle model, which shows how AI restacks workflows, organisational boundaries and ecosystems, and by the Three Horizons framework, which distinguishes immediate optimisation from deeper organisational transition and from broader ecosystem shifts. The three phases are set out below.

| | Now (H1) | Transformation phase (H2) | Long-term (H3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aim | Improve what exists | Build transitional capabilities | Re-imagine |
| Basis | Based on data and control | Based on experimentation and adaptation | Challenge core assumptions |
| Core question | How can AI improve what we already do? | How should workflows be reorganised when capabilities change? | How does AI reshape the system our organisation exists within? |
| Typical dynamics | Automation of repetitive work, productivity gains, faster research, analysis and service, better support for existing functions | AI agents in workflows, human and AI collaboration, decision support, continuous intelligence environments, new operating models | AI native organisations, new value chains, competitive advantage as ability to orchestrate and govern data |
