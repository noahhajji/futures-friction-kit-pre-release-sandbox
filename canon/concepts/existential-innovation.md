# Existential innovation

News media stand under an existential pressure. The pressure is existential because it challenges the identity of journalism and of news media: journalists have to a large degree defined themselves through their craft, such as making articles, and may now have to find other ways to fulfil their mission. The greatest existential threat arises if the connection between news medium or journalist and user, and with it relevance and economic sustainability, breaks down. That challenges the very existence of news media and quality journalism, and with it the foundation of the democratic conversation.

The investigation behind the concept points to the necessity that the pressure is not resolved through incremental development but handled through a far more fundamental approach to innovation. Existential innovation is a way to navigate that pressure.

## Definition

The definition holds two elements.

1. Fundamental innovations that secure the continued existence of the news medium, or of the individual journalist. This can cover innovation of business model, journalism, formats, technology, organisation, distribution and much else. The focus here is largely external and concrete.
2. Innovation of existence itself: who you are as a news medium, or as a journalist, and what your purpose is. Here the existential is understood more philosophically or even psychologically, but at organisational level, as innovation of, or increased clarity about, who and what one is in the new era. It concerns the medium's innermost values, core or soul. The focus here is more internal and fundamental.

The two elements interact closely, but the second, the core, is to a large extent an at times overlooked precondition for the first, the concrete innovations, leading to successful and meaningful existential innovation.

The lower field reads "Clarity about the core, fundamental existence. Foundation, internal. Values and mission." ## The five principles

Five principles together support how existential innovation can be done successfully. They are operationalised so that they answer many of the challenges journalism faces, internally and externally, technologically, commercially, organisationally and journalistically. They are relevant to media managers and editors, but also to the individual journalist, to journalism education, to unions and to other actors in the media field, and can be read at the level of the organisation and at the level of the individual.

1. Know yourself.
2. Be brave: test fast and deep, and evaluate at the same speed.
3. Use the liquid wisely, or choose "human first".
4. True truth as gold: build the new self-correcting mechanisms.
5. Deeper community and broader collaboration.

Figure: five concentric circles building outwards from the core, with principle 1 innermost and principle 5 outermost.

The principles interact and play closely together. Principle 1 is a kind of core on which the following principles build.

The existential pressure on news media and journalism mirrors a similar pressure in other sectors where AI fundamentally changes the conditions, so the strategies can, with some translation, be relevant beyond the media sector.

## How the principles relate

The principles hang together but move at the same time within opposing dimensions at the level of the medium. One way to understand the differences is to place them in external and internal categories, and to distinguish their degree of speed and movement from stabilisation and anchoring.

Figure: a four-field model inside a dashed circle. The vertical axis runs from "Acceleration (movement)" at the top to "Stabilisation (anchoring)" at the bottom; the horizontal axis from "Internal focus" on the left to "External focus" on the right. Top left, internal and acceleration: principle 2, be brave. Top right, external and acceleration: principle 3, use the liquid wisely or choose human first. Bottom left, internal and stabilisation: principle 1, know yourself. Bottom right, external and stabilisation: principle 4, true truth as gold. Along the dashed circle: principle 5, deeper community and broader collaboration.

- Know yourself (1) has largely an internal focus, is conditioned by calm, and secures stabilisation.
- Be brave (2) requires acceleration, as do the concrete experiments with liquid or human-first content (3), which also have a clear external focus.
- Developing self-correcting mechanisms (4) must interact externally, otherwise the mechanisms become worthless, while playing closely together with deep change within the medium.
- Broader collaborations and deeper communities (5) lie as a ring around the four other principles, interact with both the external and the internal, and contain both movement and anchoring.

The principles are often preconditions for one another. It is often a precondition to know oneself (1) before one can throw oneself into brave experiments (2), which can then lead to liquid or human-first content (3), which in turn can hold proposals for the new self-correcting principles that become so important for journalism (4). Brave choices and bets on 3a or 3b can also ensure that news media and journalists avoid the risky middle position that the barbell strategy warns against. All parts of the four steps play closely together with the creation of deeper communities and may require broader collaborations (5).

Figure: a step model inside a dashed circle. At the bottom "1. Know yourself", with an arrow up to "2. Be brave", from which two arrows split up towards "3a. Use the liquid wisely" on the left and "3b. Human first" on the right; from both, arrows point on up to "4. True truth as gold" at the top. In the middle between the four arrows stands the word "Collapse" over a cross that marks the risky middle position. Along the dashed circle: "5. Deeper community and broader collaboration".

For some media, individual principles will be far more relevant than others, and important elements are certainly missing. But it is a beginning.

## Recommendations to news media and journalists

Explore the five principles of existential innovation.

1. How will you define your core, do colleagues and users agree with that definition, and does it perhaps require development of the core itself?
2. How will you experiment and evaluate, and do you have the courage for it?
3. How will you work with liquid content, are you moving from content containers to content as atoms, and how do you approach human-first content strategically? Are the voices clear and genuine enough to create relationships?
4. Can you build the new self-correcting mechanisms of the era? If content becomes infrastructure, verification becomes the product. Can you develop formats for that within a new solution and intelligence economy, so that truth actually becomes gold?
5. How do you position yourself in relation to creating communities and collaborations? How will you ensure that you do not merely become a content factory for the tech giants but also build direct relationships with users, and can you help build alternative layers, formats or interfaces that deliver trustworthy knowledge directly to people, even if it requires unseen collaborations and difficult compromises?

Innovation is not a technical discipline. It is an existential necessity.
