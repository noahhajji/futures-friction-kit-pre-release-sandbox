# Futures-driven innovation model

Using the possible futures to inform innovation and strategic choices. The model places current patterns and behaviour at the centre of a timeline that runs into the past on one side and into the possible futures on the other, and draws two movements across it.

## The three parts

- Contexts and structures from the past, on the left.
- Current patterns and behaviour, at the centre.
- Possible futures with systemic changes and new logics, on the right, opening out from the present as a cone.

## The two movements

- Using knowledge of the past to inform patterns: the contexts and structures from the past narrow towards the present and explain the patterns and behaviour found there.
- Using the possible futures to inform the present decisions: the possible futures loop back to the present, so that innovation and strategic choices are made with them in view.

## Figure: the futures-driven innovation model

A horizontal timeline with arrowheads at both ends. On the left, a wide cylinder labelled "Contexts and structures from the past" narrows, through a smaller ring and dashed lines, towards a circle at the centre labelled "Current patterns & behaviour"; a curved arrow beneath this section reads "Using knowledge of the past to inform patterns". From the central circle a cone opens to the right, ending in a curved rim with a dashed outer edge, labelled "Possible futures with systemic changes and new logics"; inside the cone, circles of different sizes sit at different distances from the present, each joined to it by a dashed line. A dashed arc runs from the far rim of the cone back under the diagram to the central circle, with a curved arrow and the bold label "Using the possible futures to inform the present decisions". The source line reads: CIFS, inspired by Malte Tobias Kähler and Alina Seelbach (2024), Strategic Customer Foresight Management, and Eller, Hoffman, Schwarz (2020).
