# Communicating foresight work

Communication is key to ensuring that foresight work becomes impactful, and is not merely seen as interesting. The people it is communicated to often have not had the same rich experience of the future as those who did the work, unless they were part of the process. Effective communication speaks to the heart, the head and the hands of the audience.

## Heart, head and hands

- Heart: does the narrative about the future engage emotionally? Does it excite or alarm? Does it have an 'aha' moment, and does it provide fresh perspectives?
- Head: does the narrative add up? Does it create a reason to believe? Does it stand up to highly critical examination?
- Hands: does the narrative have relevance for decision-making? Is it actionable now, or at least in the next strategy or planning cycle?

## Matching the intended users

- The articulation and presentation of scenarios depends very much on the intended users. Scenarios that stay at the level of broad generalities, lacking supporting analysis and quantification, are not very operational and are not found useful by decision-makers, though the general public may appreciate them as a taste of the future. Scenarios presented in extremely technical and formalised ways may be hard for ordinary readers to assimilate.
- For scenarios to be used effectively, participants must be convinced of the soundness, relevance and value of the process. The foundations on which scenarios are built, the structures they use and the reasoning they employ must stand up to highly critical examination. Only then is there a chance that they will contribute to decisions and actions.
