# Strategic foresight

A discipline that involves systematically anticipating and preparing for future challenges and opportunities in ways that expand conventional strategic thinking in the face of change. It typically deals with the medium- to long-term future (beyond the current strategic planning horizon) with the overarching goal of enhancing awareness of change and considering multiple alternative futures in ways that generate valuable insights for better strategic anticipation.

## What foresight aims for

- The aim of foresight is never to predict the future.
- In a time where forecasts, projections and linear decision-making methods are not enough, strategic foresight aims to achieve a high-quality view of how the future might show up in the form of new assumptions, behaviours and realities.
- In this sense, the outset for practising foresight should always be to challenge the tendency to favour the 'business as usual' future and to adequately explore viable alternatives.

## Foresight and strategy

- Without foresight, strategy risks becoming blind to contextual change and a recipe for failure when change occurs.
- Conversely, without connection to strategic or decision-making purpose, foresight easily becomes conjecture and disconnected from organisational reality.

## Foresight is not a search for certainty

- Organisations should not engage in foresight to look for certainty. Rather the opposite: they should engage in it to challenge the bias and assumptions that could underpin a false sense of certainty and understanding.
- The main problem arises when organisations 'force fit' foresight into their current strategic practices rather than using it to balance their strategic thinking. Approached that way, foresight ends up reinforcing the original view of the future and fortifying a false sense of certainty.

## How foresight feeds strategy

- Foresight provides insights and input for strategic planning; foresight outcomes are not strategies in themselves. Strategic foresight focuses on potential changes in the external environment and allows alternative futures to be considered, which in turn influence the strategic environment and strategic decisions.
- The foresight process runs from framing and inputs, through scanning and analysis, interpreting and prospecting, to foresight outputs. Those outputs feed into strategy and policy inputs alongside other strategic considerations, and from there into strategy and policy thinking, formulation and planning.
- Foresight and strategy are two halves that come together in order to use the future in a systematic and meaningful way. Exploring the future can be insightful in itself, but its value for strategic planning or policy anticipation lies in its application.
