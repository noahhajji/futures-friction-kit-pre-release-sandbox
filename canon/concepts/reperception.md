# Reperception

The process of shifting perspective to think of the world and of the future in a new way, recognising that the future can be different from the past or from what one currently expects.

- One of the main goals of futurists and foresight practitioners is to facilitate reperception, for example by utilising scenarios or other foresight tools.
- Reperception is a key element in developing systems and strategies that can respond to emergent futures in the face of uncertainty.

## Origin

- The term comes from Pierre Wack, one of the pioneers of strategic foresight and the first to develop the use of scenario planning in the private sector, as head of strategy at a major oil company. He defined reperception as awakening to the possibility of the future being different from the past, or from how one expects it to be.
- He also held that the most important and most difficult task for people working with strategic foresight is to facilitate this transition: helping people think of the future in new ways. This is why the foresight process is at least as important as its final outcomes. The future is not a destination but a tool, and the process should develop learning, broaden participants' horizons and build a shared understanding of potential outcomes.

## Reframing to create reperception

- What scenarios essentially do is reframe in order to create reperception, in contrast to 'predict and control' approaches. We learn about the present through the lens of the future, rather than understanding the future from the perspective of the present.
- Reframing makes people mindful of the frame they have been using to make sense of the world and the future, the 'official future', and equally of what is left out of that frame. It occurs when alternative, plausible future outcomes are contrasted to reveal, test and redefine the given frame, after which actions can be rehearsed within the alternative frames to generate new shared knowledge and insights.
- The purpose of developing scenarios is not to make predictions but to challenge assumptions, bias and ignorance about what could be true in the future, and to counteract the tendency to believe that the future will merely reflect the past or adhere to a specific vision. Rather than accounting for every possible event leading to a scenario, the aim is reperception: an alternative plausible view that lets the organisation navigate and explore the implications.

## Memories of the future

- A neuroscientist has proposed that it is possible to proactively engage with the future and 'memorise' it by rehearsing it through futures thinking, so that when one is confronted with change not previously encountered, it nonetheless exists in some form in one's memories of the future: building a repertoire for future use. Scenario planning can be understood as such a rehearsal for leaders.
